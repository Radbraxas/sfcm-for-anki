# Spanish past tenses: preterite or imperfect?

Spanish has two simple past tenses, and choosing between them depends on how the speaker views the action, not only on when it happened.

The preterite (pretérito indefinido) presents an action as complete, with a clear beginning or end. It is used for single events, for a sequence of events that move a story forward, and for actions limited to a stated period. Example: "Ayer comí paella" (Yesterday I ate paella). "Viví en Madrid dos años" (I lived in Madrid for two years) uses the preterite because the period is bounded.

The imperfect (pretérito imperfecto) presents an action as ongoing or habitual, without focusing on its end. It is used for background descriptions, habits ("used to"), age, time of day and states of mind in the past. Example: "De niño, comía paella los domingos" (As a child, I used to eat paella on Sundays). "Eran las tres" (It was three o'clock) always uses the imperfect.

When both appear in one sentence, the imperfect usually sets the scene and the preterite interrupts it: "Leía cuando sonó el teléfono" (I was reading when the phone rang).

Some verbs change meaning in the preterite. "Conocí a Ana" means "I met Ana", while "conocía a Ana" means "I knew Ana". "Supe la verdad" means "I found out the truth", while "sabía la verdad" means "I knew the truth". "No quise ir" means "I refused to go", while "no quería ir" means "I didn't want to go".

Regular imperfect endings are -aba for -ar verbs (hablaba) and -ía for -er and -ir verbs (comía, vivía). Only three verbs are irregular in the imperfect: ser (era), ir (iba) and ver (veía).
